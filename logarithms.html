Logarithms

SS1 General Mathematics — First Term

What is a Logarithm?

A logarithm tells us the power to which a number must be raised to obtain another number.

For example:

2³ = 8

Therefore:

log₂ 8 = 3

This means that 2 must be raised to the power 3 to get 8.

Parts of a Logarithm

In log₂ 8 = 3:

  • 2 is the base.
  • 8 is the number being operated on.
  • 3 is the logarithm or exponent.

More Examples

10² = 100, therefore log₁₀ 100 = 2.

3² = 9, therefore log₃ 9 = 2.

5³ = 125, therefore log₅ 125 = 3.

Quick Revision

  • A logarithm is the inverse of an exponent.
  • log₂ 8 = 3 because 2³ = 8.
  • log₁₀ 100 = 2 because 10² = 100.
  • The base must be positive and not equal to 1.
